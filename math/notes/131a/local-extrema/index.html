MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Local extrema

thmanalysis/differentiation

Definition

For f:D→R, x0∈D is a local maximum point if ∃r>0 ∀x∈D∩(x0−r,x0+r): f(x)≤f(x0). Reverse the inequality for a local minimum.

Fermat's theorem

If x0 is an interior point of D, f is differentiable at x0, and x0 is a local max/min, then f′(x0)=0.

Proof at a maximum: right-hand difference quotients are ≤0, left-hand ones ≥0. Their common limit must be 0.

Hypotheses and converse

  • f′(x0)=0 is necessary under the theorem's hypotheses, but not sufficient: x3 at 0.
  • Endpoints need not have zero derivative: f(x)=x has extrema at 0,1 on [0,1].
  • A local extremum need not be differentiable: |x| at 0.
