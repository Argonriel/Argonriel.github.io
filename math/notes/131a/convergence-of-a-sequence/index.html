MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Convergence of a sequence

defanalysis/sequences

Definition

A real sequence (xn) converges to x∈R if ∀ε>0 ∃N∈N ∀n≥N: |xn−x|<ε. Write xn→x or limn→∞xn=x.

In a metric space, use d(xn,x) in place of |xn−x| and require x∈X.

Infinite limits for real sequences

xn→+∞⟺∀M∈R ∃N ∀n≥N: xn>M. xn→−∞⟺∀M∈R ∃N ∀n≥N: xn<M. These are extended-real limits, not convergence to a point of R.

Key facts

  • Every convergent sequence is bounded. ^fact-conv-bounded
  • The limit is unique.
  • Convergent ⟹ Cauchy in every metric space; the converse requires completeness.
