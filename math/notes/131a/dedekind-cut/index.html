MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Dedekind cut

defanalysis/completeness

Definition (lower cut)

A Dedekind cut is S⊂Q such that:

  1. ∅≠S≠Q;
  2. r∈S, q∈Q, q≤r⟹q∈S;
  3. S has no maximum.

Examples: a rational a corresponds to {q∈Q:q<a}. The cut S={q∈Q:q<0 or q2<2} represents 2, even though 2∉Q.

Upper-cut convention: instead use a nonempty proper upward-closed subset of Q with no minimum. This is an alternative convention; the complement of a lower cut need not satisfy it (for rational a, the complement has minimum a).

Payoff: the collection of lower cuts, with the appropriate order and arithmetic, constructs R. This realizes the Completeness Axiom; a construction is not itself a completeness axiom.
