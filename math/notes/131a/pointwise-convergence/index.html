MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Pointwise convergence

defanalysis/functions

Definition

For real-valued functions fn,f on S, fn→f pointwise⟺∀x∈S ∀ε>0 ∃N=N(ε,x) ∀n≥N:|fn(x)−f(x)|<ε.

Fix x first: each point can converge at its own rate. Uniform convergence requires the same N(ε) for every x.

Example: fn(x)=xn on [0,1] gives f(x)={0,0≤x<1,1,x=1. Every fn is continuous; f is not. Moreover sup[0,1]|fn−f|=1, so convergence is not uniform.

Pointwise convergence alone does not preserve continuity, Riemann integrability, or differentiability. Any exchange of limits with integrals or derivatives needs an additional theorem and its hypotheses.
