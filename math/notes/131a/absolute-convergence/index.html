MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Absolute convergence

defanalysis/series

Definitions

∑an is absolutely convergent if ∑|an|<∞. It is conditionally convergent if it converges but ∑|an|=∞.

Theorem (real or complex series)

Absolute convergence ⟹ convergence.

Proof: |∑k=n+1mak|≤∑k=n+1m|ak|⟶0uniformly for m>n→∞. Apply Cauchy criterion for series.

Converse fails: ∑(−1)n/n converges by Alternating Series Test, while ∑1/n diverges.

Rearrangements: absolute convergence preserves the sum under every rearrangement. For a conditionally convergent real series, a rearrangement can change the sum or make it diverge; it need not do so for every rearrangement.
