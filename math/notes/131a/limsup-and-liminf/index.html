MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

limsup & liminf

defanalysis/sequences

For a real sequence (sn), take sup/inf in [−∞,+∞]:

lim supn→∞sn=limn→∞supm≥nsm=infnsupm≥nsm, lim infn→∞sn=limn→∞infm≥nsm=supninfm≥nsm.

Tail suprema decrease and tail infima increase, so both extended-real limits always exist.

Finite convergence criterion

For L∈R, sn→L⟺lim infsn=lim supsn=L. Equality at +∞ or −∞ means an infinite limit, not convergence in R.

Subsequential limits

For a bounded real sequence, let S be its finite subsequential limits. Then ∅≠S,minS=lim infsn,maxS=lim supsn. Without boundedness, finite subsequential limits can miss escape to infinity; see Subsequence.

Example: sn=(−1)n gives S={−1,1}, lim inf=−1, lim sup=1.
