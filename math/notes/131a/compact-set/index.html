Compact set

defanalysis/topology

Let K be a subset of a metric space (X,d).

Definition (open cover)

K is compact if every cover by open sets in X has a finite subcover: K⊆⋃α∈AUα⟹∃α1,…,αm:K⊆⋃j=1mUαj.

Sequential characterization (metric spaces)

K is compact iff every sequence in K has a subsequence converging to a point in K.

Heine–Borel (Euclidean space)

K⊆Rd:K compact⟺K closed and bounded.

For general metric spaces, compact ⟹ closed and bounded, but the converse fails. An infinite set with discrete metric d(x,y)=1 for x≠y is closed and bounded in itself, yet its singleton open cover has no finite subcover.

Stability: finite unions of compact sets are compact; a relatively closed subset of a compact set is compact; a nonempty finite family of compact sets has compact intersection.

Compactness + continuity

Let f:K→Y be continuous, with Y metric.

  • Continuous image: f(K) is compact. Pull an open cover back by f, select a finite subcover of K, then map it forward.
  • Extreme Value Theorem: if K≠∅ and f:K→R, f attains a maximum and minimum. Indeed, f(K) is nonempty, closed and bounded in R, so it contains its sup and inf.
  • Heine–Cantor: f is uniformly continuous on K.

Proof map and counterexamples: Compactness and continuity.
