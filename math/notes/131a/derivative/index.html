MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Derivative

defanalysis/differentiation

Definition

For f:(a,b)→R and x∈(a,b), f′(x)=limh→0f(x+h)−f(x)h, if this finite limit exists. Then f is differentiable at x.

Geometric meaning: tangent slope / instantaneous rate of change.

Differentiability implies continuity: f(x+h)−f(x)=h(f′(x)+o(1))→0. The converse fails for f(x)=|x| at 0.

Monotonicity via MVT: for differentiable f on an interval,

  • f′≥0⟹f nondecreasing; f′>0⟹f strictly increasing.
  • f′≤0⟹f nonincreasing; f′<0⟹f strictly decreasing.
  • f′≡0⟹f constant.

Strictly increasing does not require f′>0 everywhere: x3 has derivative 0 at 0.
