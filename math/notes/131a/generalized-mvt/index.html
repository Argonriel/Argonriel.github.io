Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem

Let a<b, and let f,g:[a,b]→R be continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b). Then some c∈(a,b) satisfies f′(c)(g(b)−g(a))=g′(c)(f(b)−f(a)).

Proof: apply Rolle's Theorem to h(x)=(g(b)−g(a))f(x)−(f(b)−f(a))g(x),h(a)=h(b).

For g(x)=x, this becomes MVT. If g′≠0 everywhere on (a,b), Rolle also gives g(b)≠g(a), so division yields f′(c)g′(c)=f(b)−f(a)g(b)−g(a). 原备注：写成相除的形式，需要注意两边分母都不是 0；相乘的版本没有这个要求。This theorem underlies proofs of L'Hôpital's rule (with that rule's additional hypotheses).

Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder

Supplement: one convenient sufficient hypothesis is f∈Cn+1([a,b]), a<b. Then some c∈(a,b) satisfies f(b)=∑k=0nf(k)(a)k!(b−a)k+f(n+1)(c)(n+1)!(b−a)n+1. For n=1: f(b)=f(a)+f′(a)(b−a)+f″(c)2(b−a)2. A finite Taylor formula is not yet equality with an infinite Taylor series; that requires the remainders to tend to zero.
