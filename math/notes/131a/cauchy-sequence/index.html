MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Cauchy sequence

defanalysis/sequences

Definition

A sequence in a metric space (X,d) is Cauchy if ∀ε>0 ∃N ∀m,n≥N: d(xn,xm)<ε. In R, d(xn,xm)=|xn−xm|.

Intuition: terms eventually get close to each other; the definition does not name a limit.

By the Completeness Axiom, in R:Cauchy⟺convergent. In any metric space: convergent ⟹ Cauchy ⟹ bounded. The first converse holds in a complete space; boundedness alone gives neither Cauchy nor convergence.

Counterexamples: (−1)n is bounded but not Cauchy; rational approximations to 2 are Cauchy in Q but do not converge there.
