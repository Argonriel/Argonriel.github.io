MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Continuity

defanalysis/continuity

Let f:E→R, E⊆R, and fix x0∈E.

Definition

f is continuous at x0 if ∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 ∀x∈E:|x−x0|<δ⟹|f(x)−f(x0)|<ε.

δ may depend on ε and the fixed x0, but must work for every x in that neighborhood. Continuity on E means continuity at every x0∈E.

Sequential criterion: for every (xn)⊆E, xn→x0⟹f(xn)→f(x0). For general metrics and the open-preimage criterion, see Continuity in metric spaces.

Algebra: continuous real-valued f,g on E give continuous f+g, fg, and f/g wherever g≠0. If g:E→F and h:F→R are continuous, so is h∘g.

Compact domain: a continuous image of a compact set is compact. If the domain is also nonempty and the map real-valued, it attains max/min. Heine–Cantor supplies one δ for all base points.

Counterexample to continuity ⇒ uniform continuity: f(x)=1/x on (0,1); take xn=1/(n+1) and yn=1/(n+2). Their distance tends to 0, but |f(xn)−f(yn)|=1.
