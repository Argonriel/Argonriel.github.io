MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Subsequence

defanalysis/sequences

Definition

Choose n1<n2<⋯ in N. Then (xnk) is a subsequence of (xn).

  • If xn→x, every subsequence converges to x. ^fact-subseq-limit
  • Every real sequence has a monotone subsequence: infinitely many peaks give a nonincreasing subsequence; after the last peak, repeatedly choose a later larger term.

Here a peak is n such that xn≥xm for all m>n.

Subsequential limits

S(xn)={t∈R:xnk→t for some subsequence}.

For a bounded real sequence: xn→L⟺S(xn)={L}, minS=lim infxn,maxS=lim supxn.

Why boundedness matters: x2n=0,x2n−1=n. Then S={0}, but (xn) diverges and lim supxn=+∞.

Proof of the singleton criterion: if xn↛L, some subsequence stays at distance ≥ε from L. Boundedness and Bolzano–Weierstrass Theorem give a convergent subsubsequence whose limit is not L, a contradiction.
