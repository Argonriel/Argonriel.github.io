MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

Uniform convergence

defanalysis/functionsthm

Definition

For real-valued functions on S, fn⇉f⟺∀ε>0 ∃N ∀n≥N ∀x∈S:|fn(x)−f(x)|<ε.

For nonempty S, equivalently supx∈S|fn(x)−f(x)|→0. On bounded-function spaces, this is convergence in the sup metric. Uniform convergence can also be considered for unbounded functions, provided their differences satisfy the displayed condition.

Uniform ⟹ pointwise; the converse fails for xn on [0,1].

Uniform convergence preserves continuity

Theorem

On a metric domain S, if every fn is continuous and fn⇉f, then f is continuous.

Proof: fix x0∈S, ε>0. First choose one n with supS|fn−f|<ε/3. Then continuity of this fn gives δ>0 such that d(x,x0)<δ implies |f(x)−f(x0)|≤|f(x)−fn(x)|+|fn(x)−fn(x0)|+|fn(x0)−f(x0)|<ε.

The order is choose n, then choose δ. More preservation results: Uniform convergence theorems.
