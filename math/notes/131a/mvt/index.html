MATH 131A · Real Analysis I

MVT

thmanalysis/differentiation

Theorem

Let a<b, f:[a,b]→R be continuous on [a,b] and differentiable on (a,b). Then ∃c∈(a,b):f′(c)=f(b)−f(a)b−a.

Geometric meaning: some tangent is parallel to the secant from a to b.

Proof: set g(x)=f(x)−f(b)−f(a)b−a(x−a). Then g(a)=g(b)=f(a). By Rolle's Theorem, 0=g′(c)=f′(c)−f(b)−f(a)b−a.

Personal note: 咋学了两次也没记住，总之 g(a)=g(b)=f(a)。

Generalized MVT compares the changes in two functions, replacing the denominator b−a by g(b)−g(a) when the required denominators are nonzero.
