MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Complex numbers and polar form

defcomplex-analysis/basics

For z=x+iy, with x,y∈R, z―=x−iy,|z|=x2+y2,zz―=|z|2.

Polar form

For z≠0, write z=reiθ, where r=|z|>0 and eiθ=cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ. All arguments differ by 2πk, k∈Z; choose Arg⁡z∈(−π,π] as the principal argument.

Multiplication multiplies moduli and adds arguments: r1eiθ1r2eiθ2=r1r2ei(θ1+θ2). Thus (eiθ)n=einθ for n∈Z. If n≥1 and z=reiθ≠0, its n distinct nth roots are r1/nei(θ+2πk)/n,k=0,…,n−1. For n≥2, the nth roots of unity sum to 0, by the finite geometric sum.

Useful estimates: |z+w|≤|z|+|w|,||z|−|w||≤|z−w|. Equality in the first holds exactly when one number is zero or the two nonzero numbers have the same argument modulo 2π.

Boundary: 0 has no argument. The principal argument has a jump across the negative real axis; choosing a principal value does not make it globally continuous.
