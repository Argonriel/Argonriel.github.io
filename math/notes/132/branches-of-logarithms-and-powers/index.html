MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Branches of logarithms and powers

defcomplex-analysis/basics

For z≠0, all solutions of ew=z are w=log⁡|z|+i(Arg⁡z+2πk),k∈Z.

Principal branch

On Ω=C∖(−∞,0], take −π<Arg⁡z<π and define Log⁡z=log⁡|z|+iArg⁡z. It is holomorphic, eLog⁡z=z, and (Log⁡z)′=1/z.

A logarithm branch on a domain D⊆C∖{0} is a holomorphic function L with eL=z. Two such branches differ by a constant 2πik. A simply connected domain avoiding 0 admits a branch: integrate 1/z and choose the additive constant appropriately.

No continuous logarithm exists on the whole punctured plane. Along z=eit, continuity would force the imaginary part of a logarithm to increase by 2π after one turn, although the endpoint is unchanged.

Once a branch L is fixed, define zα=eαL(z),(zα)′=αe(α−1)L(z). For square roots on Ω, the branches are ±eLog⁡z/2. There is no continuous global square root on the punctured plane, by the same loop argument with a sign change.

Example: all values of ii are e−(π/2+2πk), k∈Z; the principal value is e−π/2.

Boundary: logarithm and power identities depend on the branch. Even when all principal logarithms involved exist, Log⁡(zw) need not equal Log⁡z+Log⁡w.
