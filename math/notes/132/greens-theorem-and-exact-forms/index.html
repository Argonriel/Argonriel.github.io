MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Green's theorem and exact forms

defthmcomplex-analysis/integration

Green's theorem

Let D⊂R2 be a bounded region whose boundary consists of finitely many disjoint piecewise C1 simple closed curves. If P,Q are C1 on a neighborhood of D―, then ∫∂DPdx+Qdy=∬D(Qx−Py)dxdy.

The positive boundary orientation keeps D to the left: the outer boundary is counterclockwise, and boundaries of holes are clockwise.

On an open set, a C1 form ω=Pdx+Qdy is closed if Py=Qx. It is exact if ω=dF for a potential F, so Fx=P, Fy=Q.

Exact forms are closed. On a simply connected domain, closed C1 forms are exact. Exactness is equivalent to path independence and to zero integral around every piecewise smooth closed path in the domain.

Counterexample: on R2∖{0}, ω=−ydx+xdyx2+y2 is closed, but its integral around the positive unit circle is 2π. Therefore it is not exact there.

For a holomorphic f=u+iv, both udx−vdy and vdx+udy are closed by Cauchy-Riemann. This connects real line integrals to Cauchy's theorem.
