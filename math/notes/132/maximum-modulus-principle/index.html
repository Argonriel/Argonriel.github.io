Maximum modulus principle

thmcomplex-analysis/holomorphic

Maximum modulus

If f is holomorphic and nonconstant on a domain D, then |f| has no local maximum in D.

Proof idea: suppose |f| has a local maximum at a. If f(a)=0, then f=0 nearby. Otherwise choose λ with |λ|=1 and λf(a)=|f(a)|. Near a, Re⁡(λf(z))≤|f(z)|≤|f(a)|. The real part is harmonic, so its maximum principle forces it to be constant. Cauchy-Riemann then forces f to be constant; connectedness propagates the conclusion.

If D is bounded and f∈C(D―) is holomorphic in D, then maxD―|f|=max∂D|f|.

Minimum modulus: if f is zero-free on D, apply the maximum principle to 1/f. A nonconstant zero-free holomorphic function has no interior local minimum of its modulus. The corresponding boundary minimum statement requires f continuous and zero-free on D―.

Boundary: f(z)=z has an interior minimum of |f| at its zero. Holomorphicity of f does not mean |f| is harmonic; the proof uses a rotated real part or 1/f.
