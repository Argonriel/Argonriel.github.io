MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Real integrals by residues

examplecomplex-analysis/residues

The source example evaluates, for real a, ∫−∞∞cos⁡(ax)1+x2dx=πe−|a|. The improper integral is absolutely convergent because the integrand is bounded in modulus by 1/(1+x2).

For a≥0: take f(z)=eiaz1+z2 on the positively oriented upper half-disk of radius R>1. Its boundary is the segment from −R to R, followed by the upper semicircle ΓR from R to −R.

The only enclosed pole is i, with residue e−a/(2i), so ∫−RReiax1+x2dx+∫ΓRf(z)dz=πe−a.

On the upper semicircle, |eiaz|=e−aIm⁡z≤1 and |1+z2|≥R2−1. Therefore |∫ΓRf(z)dz|≤πRR2−1⟶0. Let R→∞ and take real parts. The estimate also covers a=0; for a<0, use cos⁡(ax)=cos⁡(|a|x).

Boundary: symmetry of the finite interval alone would give only a principal-value limit. Here absolute convergence makes it the ordinary improper integral. For other exponential contour integrals, choose the half-plane where the exponential decays and prove the closing arc tends to zero; residues alone do not establish that step.
