MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Poles and meromorphic functions

defthmcomplex-analysis/singularities

Pole

A function holomorphic on a punctured neighborhood has a pole of order m≥1 at a iff f(z)=g(z)(z−a)m, where g is holomorphic near a and g(a)≠0.

This is equivalent to 1/f having a zero of order m at a, after restricting to a smaller punctured disk and extending the reciprocal at a. It also implies |f(z)|→∞.

Conversely, if |f(z)|→∞ at an isolated singularity, then 1/f→0 extends holomorphically. Its zero has finite order, so f has a pole.

A function is meromorphic on a domain if it is holomorphic except at a discrete set of poles. These poles have no accumulation point inside the domain. Holomorphic functions are included as the case with no poles.

If g,h are holomorphic, h is not identically zero on the domain, and removable points are filled in, then g/h is meromorphic. If their finite zero orders at a are m,n, respectively, cancellation gives a pole of order n−m when n>m, and a removable point when n≤m.

Example: (z2−1)/(z−1) has a removable point at 1 with extension z+1, whereas 1/(z−1)2 has a double pole. A zero of a written denominator need not survive cancellation.
