MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Locally uniform convergence

defthmcomplex-analysis/series

Uniform on compact subsets

Functions fn on an open set U converge locally uniformly to f if supz∈K|fn(z)−f(z)|→0 for every compact K⊂U.

The lecture calls this normal convergence of a sequence. Here that term means compact uniform convergence; for series, some texts use normal convergence for the stronger condition of locally summable sup norms.

Example: zn→0 locally uniformly on D, since sup|z|≤r|zn|=rn→0 for r<1. It does not converge uniformly on the whole disk, where the supremum is always 1.

Holomorphic limit theorem

If each fn is holomorphic on U and fn→f locally uniformly, then f is holomorphic and fn(m)→f(m) locally uniformly for every m≥1.

Use Morera for holomorphicity. For derivatives, choose 0<r<s with D(a,s)―⊂U. Cauchy's formula gives sup|z−a|≤r|fn(m)(z)−f(m)(z)|≤m!s(s−r)m+1sup|w−a|=s|fn(w)−f(w)|. Cover a compact set by finitely many smaller disks.

Boundary: in real analysis, uniform convergence of differentiable functions alone does not ensure convergence of their derivatives. Here holomorphicity and the room for a surrounding contour supply the stronger conclusion.
