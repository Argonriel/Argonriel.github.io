MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Operations on power series

thmcomplex-analysis/series

Suppose f(z)=∑n≥0anzn and g(z)=∑n≥0bnzn converge on |z|<R, with R>0.

On this common disk, f(z)+g(z)=∑n≥0(an+bn)zn, f(z)g(z)=∑n≥0cnzn,cn=∑j=0najbn−j. Absolute convergence justifies the Cauchy product and rearrangement.

If b0≠0, write g=b0(1+B) with B(0)=0. On a sufficiently small disk, |B|<1, so 1g=1b0∑k=0∞(−B)k. Equivalently, if 1/g=∑cnzn, then c0=1b0,cn=−1b0∑j=1nbjcn−j(n≥1).

Example from the notes: near 0, ez1+z=(1+z+z22+z36+O(z4))(1−z+z2−z3+O(z4))=1+z22−z33+O(z4). The full Taylor series has radius 1, because −1 is a genuine pole.

Boundary: g(0)≠0 ensures a local reciprocal, not a zero-free original disk. The geometric expression requires |B(z)|<1 where it is used; the reciprocal may extend further by other means.
