MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Complex power series

defthmcomplex-analysis/series

For an,a∈C, consider ∑n=0∞an(z−a)n.

Radius of convergence

R=1lim supn→∞|an|1/n,1/0=∞,1/∞=0.

The series converges absolutely for |z−a|<R, and diverges for |z−a|>R. At z=a it always equals a0, even if R=0. For 0<R<∞, points on the boundary circle require separate examination.

If R>0, convergence is uniform on each closed disk |z−a|≤r<R, hence locally uniform on D(a,R). Choose r<s<R; boundedness of |an|sn gives a geometric majorant C(r/s)n, and apply the M-test.

The sum is holomorphic, and termwise differentiation gives f(m)(z)=∑n=m∞n!(n−m)!an(z−a)n−m. Each differentiated series has the same radius and converges locally uniformly inside it.

Examples: ∑zn and ∑nzn have radius 1; ∑zn/n! has infinite radius. Convergence on every smaller closed disk does not assert uniform convergence on the entire open convergence disk.

Compare the real-variable card: an interval has two endpoints to check, while a complex disk has a whole boundary circle.
