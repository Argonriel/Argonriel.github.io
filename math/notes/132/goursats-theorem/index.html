MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Goursat's theorem

thmcomplex-analysis/integration

Goursat

If f is complex differentiable at every point of an open set U, then its integral around the boundary of every closed rectangle contained in U is zero. No continuity assumption on f′ is needed.

Proof idea: subdivide a rectangle into four congruent rectangles. Internal sides cancel, so one subrectangle has boundary integral of modulus at least one quarter of the original. Repeat to obtain nested rectangles Rn shrinking to a∈U, with |∫∂Rnfdz|≥4−n|∫∂R0fdz|.

Differentiability at a gives f(z)=f(a)+f′(a)(z−a)+ε(z)(z−a),ε(z)→0. The affine part has a primitive, so its closed integral vanishes. Both the perimeter and diameter of Rn shrink by 2−n; the ML estimate bounds the remaining integral by C4−nsupz∈Rn|ε(z)|. Compare the two bounds and let n→∞.

Complex differentiability already implies continuity of f. The local primitive construction then leads to Cauchy's formula, and hence to continuous derivatives of all orders and local power-series expansions.

Role: the lecture notes first impose continuity of the derivative. Goursat shows that condition is redundant in the final definition of holomorphicity.
