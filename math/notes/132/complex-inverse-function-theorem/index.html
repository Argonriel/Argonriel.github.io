MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Complex inverse function theorem

thmcomplex-analysis/holomorphic

Local inverse

Suppose f is holomorphic near a and f′(a)≠0. There are neighborhoods U of a and V of f(a) such that f:U→V is bijective and its inverse is holomorphic. (f−1)′(w)=1f′(f−1(w))(w∈V).

Write f′=p+iq. The Cauchy-Riemann equations give the real Jacobian Df=(p−qqp),detDf=p2+q2=|f′|2. Use the real inverse function theorem. For w=f(z) and w0=f(z0), f−1(w)−f−1(w0)w−w0=z−z0f(z)−f(z0)⟶1f′(z0).

The same matrix is a rotation followed by scaling when f′≠0: this explains local angle preservation at regular points.

Example: ez has nonzero derivative everywhere. Each local inverse is a logarithm branch, with derivative 1/z.

Boundary: ez+2πi=ez, so nonzero derivative everywhere does not imply global injectivity. The inverse theorem requires f holomorphic on a neighborhood, not merely differentiable at the single point.
