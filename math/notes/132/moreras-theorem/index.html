MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Morera's theorem

thmcomplex-analysis/integration

Morera

Let f be continuous on an open set U. If ∫∂Rf(z)dz=0 for every closed rectangle R⊂U with sides parallel to the coordinate axes, then f is holomorphic on U.

A standard equivalent version uses boundaries of all triangles contained in U. These are local tests; simple connectedness of U is not required.

Proof idea: work in a small rectangle. Define F(z) by integrating f from a fixed point along horizontal and vertical segments. The zero-rectangle condition makes the two orders of travel agree. Continuity yields Fx=f,Fy=if. Thus F is holomorphic and F′=f. Cauchy's formula shows that the derivative of a holomorphic function is holomorphic, so f is holomorphic.

Use for limits: if holomorphic fn converge uniformly on compact subsets of U, the limit is continuous. For a rectangle compactly contained in U, the ML estimate lets the limit pass through the boundary integral, giving zero; apply Morera.

Boundary: continuity is a hypothesis, not a consequence of the integral condition alone. The function equal to 1 at one point and 0 elsewhere has zero integrals around rectangle boundaries but is not continuous at that point.
