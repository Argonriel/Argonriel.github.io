Residue theorem for a region

Let D be a bounded region whose boundary is a finite union of disjoint piecewise C1 simple closed curves. Suppose f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of D― except for finitely many isolated singularities a1,…,aN inside D. Then ∫∂Df(z)dz=2πi∑j=1NRes⁡(f,aj).

Use the full positive boundary: the region stays to the left. Singularities may be poles or essential singularities; there must be none on the integration boundary.

Proof: remove small disjoint closed disks around the singularities. The function is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the remaining region. Cauchy's theorem gives ∫∂Dfdz=∑j=1N∫|z−aj|=rjf(z)dz, where the small circles on the right are counterclockwise. Each circle integral equals 2πi times its residue.

Example: for a counterclockwise unit circle, ∫|z|=1e1/zdz=2πi, even though 0 is essential rather than a pole.

Boundary: the displayed formula is for the full boundary of a region. A path traversed twice gives twice the integral, and reversing it changes the sign; arbitrary closed paths require corresponding winding-number weights. A pole on the contour cannot be included by direct substitution into this theorem.
