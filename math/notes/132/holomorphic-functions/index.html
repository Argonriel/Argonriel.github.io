MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Holomorphic functions

defcomplex-analysis/holomorphic

Let U⊆C be open and a∈U.

Complex derivative

f′(a)=limh→0f(a+h)−f(a)h, if the limit exists for complex increments h≠0.

Equivalently, f(a+h)=f(a)+f′(a)h+o(|h|). Complex differentiability implies continuity, and the usual sum, product, quotient and chain rules hold where their expressions are defined.

A function is holomorphic on U if it is complex differentiable at every point of U; it is entire if U=C. By Goursat and Cauchy's formula, it then has continuous derivatives of all orders and is locally a convergent power series. In one complex variable, analytic and holomorphic therefore agree.

Examples: polynomials and ez are entire; rational functions are holomorphic away from denominator zeros. The function z― is nowhere complex differentiable: real increments give quotient 1, imaginary increments give −1.

Boundary: |z|2 is complex differentiable at 0, with derivative 0, but is holomorphic on no neighborhood of 0.

On a domain, f′≡0 implies f is constant: its real and imaginary gradients vanish, and connect points by polygonal paths. Connectedness is needed to obtain one constant on the whole set.
