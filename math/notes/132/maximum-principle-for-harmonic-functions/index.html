MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Maximum principle for harmonic functions

thmcomplex-analysis/harmonic

Strong maximum and minimum principles

A nonconstant real harmonic function on a domain has no interior local maximum or minimum.

Proof route: the mean value property forces equality on a small disk around a maximizing point. Harmonic functions have local holomorphic representations, so they are real analytic; constancy propagates across the connected domain. For a global maximum, one can instead show directly that the maximum set is both open and closed. Apply the result to −u for minima.

If D is bounded and u∈C(D―) is real harmonic in D, then maxD―u=max∂Du,minD―u=min∂Du. Compactness guarantees the extrema exist; an interior extremum forces constancy.

Complex harmonic version: if h is complex harmonic and |h| has an interior maximum, rotate h so that its value there is real and nonnegative. Apply the real maximum principle to the real part and use Re⁡h≤|h|. This forces h to be constant.

Consequence: two real harmonic functions continuous on the closure of a bounded domain and agreeing on its boundary agree everywhere. Apply the maximum and minimum principles to their difference.

Boundary: boundedness matters for the boundary conclusion. On the upper half-plane, u(x,y)=y vanishes on the boundary but is positive inside. Also |h| need not itself be harmonic.
