MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Cauchy-Riemann equations

thmcomplex-analysis/holomorphic

Write f(x+iy)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y).

Cauchy-Riemann

Complex differentiability at a point implies ux=vy,uy=−vx, and f′=ux+ivx=vy−iuy there.

Proof idea: take the difference quotient first along real increments and then along imaginary increments. Both limits must equal the same complex derivative.

Conversely, if u,v are real differentiable at a point and satisfy these equations there, their real linear approximation is multiplication by ux+ivx, so f is complex differentiable there. In particular, if u,v∈C1(U) and the equations hold throughout an open set U, then f is holomorphic on U.

Why regularity matters: put u(0,0)=0, u(x,y)=xy/(x2+y2) elsewhere, and v=0. All four partial derivatives at 0 vanish, so the equations hold there, but f=u is not even continuous at 0.

For local polar coordinates z=reiθ with r>0, ur=1rvθ,uθ=−rvr. These follow from the real chain rule. They do not give a coordinate formula at r=0.

Consequence: if both f and f― are holomorphic on a domain, all first partial derivatives vanish, so f is constant.
