MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Complex limits and continuity

defcomplex-analysis/basics

For zn,z∈C, zn→z⟺|zn−z|→0⟺Re⁡zn→Re⁡z and Im⁡zn→Im⁡z. The complex plane is complete: a sequence converges iff it is Cauchy, by applying real completeness to its two coordinates.

Function limit

Let f:E→C and let a be a limit point of E. Then limz→af(z)=L means ∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 ∀z∈E:0<|z−a|<δ⟹|f(z)−L|<ε.

Equivalently, f(zn)→L for every sequence zn∈E∖{a} tending to a.

Continuity at a∈E uses the same estimate without excluding z=a, with L=f(a). At an isolated point it holds automatically. Sums and products preserve limits; quotients do when the limiting denominator is nonzero.

Boundary: agreement along the real and imaginary axes is insufficient. For f(x+iy)=xyx2+y2(x+iy≠0), both axes give 0, while the line y=x gives 1/2. A complex limit must work for every approach within the domain.
