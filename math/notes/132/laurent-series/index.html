Laurent expansion

If f is holomorphic on A={z:r<|z−a|<R}, with 0≤r<R≤∞, then it has a unique expansion f(z)=∑n=−∞∞cn(z−a)n, where, for any r<ρ<R, cn=12πi∫|w−a|=ρf(w)(w−a)n+1dw(n∈Z).

Each coefficient circle is counterclockwise. The two one-sided sums converge absolutely at each point of A and uniformly on every smaller closed annulus.

The nonnegative powers extend holomorphically to |z−a|<R. The negative powers extend holomorphically to |z−a|>r and vanish at infinity. This normalization makes the decomposition unique.

Proof route: apply Cauchy's formula between an inner and an outer circle. Expand each kernel geometrically in the appropriate ratio. Uniform convergence allows integration term by term; circle integrals of powers isolate each coefficient.

Example from the notes: f(z)=1(z−1)(z−2)=1z−2−1z−1. About 0, the expansions depend on the annulus: 0<|z|<1:∑n=0∞(1−2−n−1)zn,1<|z|<2:−∑n=0∞zn2n+1−∑n=1∞z−n,2<|z|:∑n=0∞(2n−1)z−n−1.

Boundary: coefficients are unique for a fixed annulus, not across different annuli. The negative powers in the middle expansion do not make 0 a singularity of f: that expansion does not hold arbitrarily close to 0.
