Harmonic functions

defcomplex-analysis/harmonic

Harmonicity

A real-valued function u on an open set U⊆C≃R2 is harmonic if u∈C2(U) and Δu=uxx+uyy=0.

If f=u+iv is holomorphic, both u and v are harmonic. Indeed, holomorphic functions are smooth, and Cauchy-Riemann plus equality of mixed partials gives uxx+uyy=vyx−vxy=0. The argument for v is the same.

Examples: x, y, x2−y2, xy are harmonic on the plane. The function log⁡|z| is harmonic on the punctured plane; |z|2=x2+y2 is not, since its Laplacian is 4.

Linear combinations of harmonic functions are harmonic. A complex-valued function is called harmonic when both its real and imaginary parts are harmonic; this does not imply it is holomorphic. For example, z― is complex harmonic but nowhere holomorphic.

Boundary: two harmonic functions u,v need not make u+iv holomorphic. The pair must also satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations; this is the role of a harmonic conjugate.
