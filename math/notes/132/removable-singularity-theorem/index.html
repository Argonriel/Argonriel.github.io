Riemann removable singularity theorem

Suppose f is holomorphic on 0<|z−a|<R. If it is bounded on some smaller punctured neighborhood of a, then it extends holomorphically across a.

For an isolated singularity, the following are equivalent:

  • it is removable;
  • f is bounded near a;
  • limz→af(z) exists and is finite.

The extension is unique, with its value at a equal to that limit.

Proof by coefficients: if |f|≤M near a, then for a sufficiently small positive circle radius ρ and every k≥1, |c−k|=|12πi∫|z−a|=ρf(z)(z−a)k−1dz|≤Mρk. Let ρ↓0. Every negative coefficient vanishes, leaving a Taylor series.

Example: sin⁡zz=1−z23!+z45!−⋯ extends with value 1 at 0.

Boundary: boundedness along one path is insufficient. The essential singularity e1/z tends to 0 along the negative real axis but is unbounded along the positive real axis.
