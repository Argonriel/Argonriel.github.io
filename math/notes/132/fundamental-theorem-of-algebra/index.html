Fundamental theorem of algebra

Every nonconstant polynomial with complex coefficients has a complex root. Consequently, a degree-n polynomial factors into n linear factors over C, counted with multiplicity.

Proof from Liouville: let p have positive degree and suppose it has no zero. Then 1/p is entire. The leading term gives |p(z)|→∞(|z|→∞), so 1/p is bounded outside a sufficiently large disk. Inside the closed disk it is continuous and therefore bounded by compactness. Liouville makes 1/p, hence p, constant, a contradiction. Factor out one root and repeat.

Linear algebra connection: the characteristic polynomial of an operator on a nonzero finite-dimensional complex vector space has a root, so the operator has an eigenvalue. The polynomial splits over C, allowing Jordan form; this alone does not imply diagonalizability.

Boundary: the statement depends on the field. The polynomial t2+1 has no real root, and a real rotation can have no real eigenvalue.
