MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Holomorphic functions at infinity

defcomplex-analysis/singularities

Suppose f is holomorphic on |z|>R for some R>0.

Coordinate at infinity

The function is holomorphic at infinity if g(w)=f(1/w), initially defined for 0<|w|<1/R, extends holomorphically to w=0.

Then f(∞)=g(0)=limz→∞f(z) is finite, and for all sufficiently large |z|, f(z)=b0+b1z+b2z2+⋯. The inverse-power series is obtained from the Taylor series of g and converges uniformly on compact subsets of its exterior region. Local boundedness for sufficiently large |z| is an equivalent criterion, by the removable singularity theorem applied to g.

Classification at infinity is also made through g:

  • f(z)=1/z is holomorphic at infinity, with value 0;
  • f(z)=zm, m≥1, has a pole of order m at infinity;
  • f(z)=ez has an essential singularity at infinity, since e1/w has infinitely many negative Laurent powers at 0.

Boundary: holomorphic on an exterior region does not already mean holomorphic at infinity. A finite limit is required for a complex-valued holomorphic extension in this coordinate.
