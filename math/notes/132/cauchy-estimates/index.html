MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Cauchy estimates

thmcomplex-analysis/integration

Derivative bounds

Suppose f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of D(a,r)―, with r>0, and |f(z)|≤M on |z−a|=r. Then for n≥0, |f(n)(a)|≤n!Mrn.

Proof: combine Cauchy's derivative formula with the ML estimate: |f(n)(a)|≤n!2πMrn+1(2πr).

For Taylor coefficients an=f(n)(a)/n!, this becomes |an|≤M/rn.

Use: if f is entire and globally bounded by the same M, take arbitrarily large r with n=1 to obtain f′(a)=0. This is Liouville's theorem.

More generally, if |f(z)|≤C(1+|z|m) for all z, with integer m≥0, apply the estimate at 0 and let r→∞ to see f(n)(0)=0 for n>m. The entire Taylor expansion makes f a polynomial of degree at most m.

Boundary: an arbitrary entire function has a bound Mr on each circle, but Mr can grow with r. Letting r→∞ only helps when that growth is controlled.
