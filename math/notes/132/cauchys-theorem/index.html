MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Cauchy's theorem

thmcomplex-analysis/integration

Boundary version

Let D be a bounded region whose boundary consists of finitely many disjoint piecewise C1 simple closed curves. If f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of D―, then ∫∂Df(z)dz=0.

Use the complete positive boundary: the outer curve is counterclockwise and each inner curve is clockwise. No simple connectedness is needed for this boundary statement.

Proof idea: write f=u+iv and apply Green's theorem to fdz=(udx−vdy)+i(vdx+udy). Cauchy-Riemann makes both area integrands zero. Goursat's theorem supplies the theorem without separately assuming continuity of f′.

Simply connected version: if f is holomorphic on a simply connected domain U, its integral around every piecewise smooth closed path in U is zero. Equivalently, it has a primitive there. One obtains this version from the local integral theorem by subdividing a contraction of the path.

Hole example: for 0<r<R and f(z)=1/z, 0=∫∂{r<|z|<R}dzz=∫|z|=Rdzz−∫|z|=rdzz, where both displayed circles on the right are counterclockwise.

Boundary: being holomorphic only near a closed curve is insufficient to make its integral zero. The interior obstruction at 0 gives ∫|z|=1dz/z=2πi.
