MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Cauchy integral formula

thmcomplex-analysis/integration

Let D be a bounded region whose boundary is a finite union of disjoint piecewise C1 simple closed curves. Suppose f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of D―.

Values and derivatives from the boundary

For z∈D and n≥0, f(n)(z)=n!2πi∫∂Df(w)(w−z)n+1dw.

The boundary has its full positive orientation, including clockwise boundaries of holes. The formula for n=0 determines every interior value from the boundary values.

Proof route: remove a small disk centered at z and apply Cauchy's theorem to f(w)/(w−z). The outer boundary integral equals the integral over the small counterclockwise circle. Parametrize it and let its radius tend to zero to obtain 2πif(z). Differentiating the kernel is justified uniformly on the fixed boundary, since an interior point has positive distance from it.

Example from the notes: on the positive unit circle, put h(z)=e−z/(z2−4). It is holomorphic near the closed unit disk, and h′(0)=1/4. Hence ∫|z|=1dzz2(z2−4)ez=2πih′(0)=πi2.

Boundary: the point z must be inside D, and f must be regular throughout the required region. A singularity on the contour prevents direct use of this formula.
