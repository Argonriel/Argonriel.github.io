Residue at an isolated singularity

If f(z)=∑n∈Zcn(z−a)n on a punctured disk, then Res⁡(f,a)=c−1=12πi∫|z−a|=rf(z)dz, for a sufficiently small counterclockwise circle.

The residue is defined for removable, pole and essential isolated singularities. Only the coefficient of power −1 contributes to the circle integral.

Simple pole: Res⁡(f,a)=limz→a(z−a)f(z). If g,h are holomorphic near a, with h(a)=0 and h′(a)≠0, then Res⁡(g/h,a)=g(a)h′(a). When g(a)=0, the quotient is removable and this residue is 0.

Pole of order m: let G(z)=(z−a)mf(z) extend holomorphically. Then Res⁡(f,a)=G(m−1)(a)(m−1)!. This extracts the Taylor coefficient of G that becomes power −1 after division by (z−a)m.

Examples: Res⁡(1/z2,0)=0 but Res⁡(e1/z,0)=1. For real t, Res⁡(eitz1+z2,i)=e−t2i.

Boundary: the simple-pole limit formula is not a general formula for essential singularities; use the Laurent coefficient when no finite pole order applies.
