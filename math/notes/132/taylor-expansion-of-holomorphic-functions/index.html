MATH 132 · Complex Analysis

Taylor expansion of holomorphic functions

thmcomplex-analysis/series

Holomorphic implies analytic

If f is holomorphic on D(a,R), then f(z)=∑n=0∞f(n)(a)n!(z−a)n(|z−a|<R). The series has radius at least R and converges uniformly on every smaller closed disk.

For 0<r<R, its coefficients are an=f(n)(a)n!=12πi∫|w−a|=rf(w)(w−a)n+1dw.

Proof idea: for |z−a|<r, expand the Cauchy kernel as a geometric series: 1w−z=∑n=0∞(z−a)n(w−a)n+1(|w−a|=r). The series is uniform on this fixed contour, so integrate term by term. Cauchy estimates give uniform convergence on smaller disks; let r range below R.

Examples: 11−z=∑n=0∞zn(|z|<1),ez=∑n=0∞znn!(z∈C), Log⁡(1+z)=∑n=1∞(−1)n+1nzn(|z|<1). The last identity uses the branch with Log⁡(1)=0.

Boundary: unlike a merely smooth real function, a holomorphic function cannot have all derivatives zero at an interior point without being zero nearby. The coefficients are unique; the given domain need not be the maximal domain of convergence.
