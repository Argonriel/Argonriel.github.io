MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Generalized eigenspace

deflinear-algebra/operators

Let T∈L(V), n=dim⁡V<∞, and λ∈F.

Generalized eigenvector

A nonzero v is a generalized eigenvector for λ if (T−λI)kv=0for some k≥1.

The kernels form an increasing chain that stabilizes by k=n. Thus the generalized eigenspace is Gλ=⋃k≥1ker⁡(T−λI)k=ker⁡(T−λI)n. It contains the ordinary eigenspace Eλ=ker⁡(T−λI).

Gλ is invariant, and Nλ=(T−λI)|Gλ is nilpotent. The restriction matters: T−λI need not be nilpotent on all of V.

If χT splits over F, then V=⨁λGλ,dim⁡Gλ=mλ. The factors (t−λ)mλ are pairwise coprime; polynomial Bezout identities separate the corresponding components. Each restriction is λI+Nλ, leading to Jordan canonical form.

Example: for A=(1101), E1=span⁡e1,G1=F2, and (A−I)e2=e1, (A−I)e1=0.

For a diagonalizable operator, Gλ=Eλ; generalized eigenvectors add no new directions.
