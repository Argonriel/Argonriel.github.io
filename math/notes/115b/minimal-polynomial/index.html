MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Minimal polynomial

defthmlinear-algebra/operators

Let T∈L(V), where V≠{0} is finite-dimensional over F.

Definition

The minimal polynomial mT is the monic polynomial of smallest degree such that mT(T)=0.

It exists by Cayley-Hamilton theorem. Polynomial division shows that every annihilating polynomial is divisible by mT: if p=qmT+r and p(T)=0, then r(T)=0, forcing r=0 by minimality.

In particular, mT∣χT. The two polynomials have the same irreducible factors, but generally different multiplicities.

Diagonalization criterion

T is diagonalizable over F iff mT(t)=∏j=1r(t−λj) for distinct λj∈F.

For the reverse implication, Lagrange polynomials pj(t)=∏i≠jt−λiλj−λi give projections pj(T) onto the eigenspaces, with ∑jpj(T)=I.

Examples: mI=t−1, while χIn=(t−1)n. A nontrivial 2×2 Jordan block at 1 has mT=(t−1)2. A real 90∘ rotation has mT=t2+1: no repeated roots, but no splitting over R, hence no real diagonalization.

When Jordan form exists, the exponent of t−λ in mT is the size of the largest block for λ.
