MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Positive definite matrix

defthmlinear-algebra/quadratic-forms

Let A=AT∈Mn(R).

  • Positive definite: xTAx>0 for every x≠0.
  • Positive semidefinite: xTAx≥0 for every x; this includes positive definite matrices.
  • Indefinite: the form takes both positive and negative values.

By the Spectral theorem, these correspond to all eigenvalues being positive, all being nonnegative, or having both signs. A singular positive semidefinite matrix additionally has a zero eigenvalue.

Sylvester's criterion

A real symmetric matrix is positive definite iff all its leading principal minors are positive: Δk=detA1:k,1:k>0(k=1,…,n).

Proof idea: successive completion of squares gives pivots Δk/Δk−1, with Δ0=1. Positive pivots make the diagonal form positive; conversely, every leading principal restriction of a positive definite form is positive definite.

The source's matrix (2113) has Δ1=2, Δ2=5, hence is positive definite.

Replacing by ≥ for leading minors does not characterize semidefiniteness: diag⁡(0,−1) has leading minors 0,0 but takes negative values.

For positive semidefinite A, xTAx=0 iff Ax=0. For indefinite forms this fails: x12−x22 vanishes on two lines, although its matrix is invertible. A quadratic form is not a linear map, so its zero set should not generally be called a kernel.

Positive definite A defines an inner product ⟨x,y⟩A=xTAy on Rn.
