MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Quadratic form

deflinear-algebra/quadratic-forms

A real quadratic form on Rn can be written q(x)=xTAx,A=AT. Any matrix B gives the same quadratic form as its symmetric part (B+BT)/2; the skew-symmetric part contributes zero.

Cross terms are halved: the source's example q(x,y,z)=2x2+3y2−z2+4xy−6xz+8yz has symmetric matrix A=(22−3234−34−1). Both aijxixj and ajixjxi contribute to the same cross term.

Changing coordinates

If x=Py with P invertible, then q(x)=yT(PTAP)y. The matrix changes by congruence, not general similarity.

By the real Spectral theorem, choose orthogonal Q with A=QDQT. Setting y=QTx gives q(x)=λ1y12+⋯+λnyn2. The signs of the eigenvalues determine the possible signs of q.

Completing the square: 2x2+2xy+3y2=2(x+y/2)2+52y2. This also removes cross terms, but the coordinate change need not be orthogonal. The resulting coefficients need not be eigenvalues.
