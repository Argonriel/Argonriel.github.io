MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Sylvester law of inertia

thmlinear-algebra/quadratic-forms

Let A be a real symmetric n×n matrix. Its inertia is (n+,n−,n0), the numbers of positive, negative and zero eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity. The source calls this triple the signature; some texts instead use n+−n− for that word.

Sylvester's law of inertia

For every invertible real P, A and PTAP have the same inertia.

First use the Spectral theorem to diagonalize A orthogonally. Rescale the coordinates with nonzero coefficients to obtain PTAP=diag⁡(In+,−In−,0n0).

Why the counts are intrinsic: n+ is the largest dimension of a subspace on which q(x)=xTAx is positive definite. A subspace of larger dimension intersects the span of the nonpositive eigendirections, a contradiction. The analogous argument gives n−; rank or nullity determines n0. Invertible coordinate changes preserve these dimensions.

Consequently, two real symmetric forms are congruent iff their inertia triples agree. Congruence preserves rank and signs, but generally not individual eigenvalues: diag⁡(1,−1)anddiag⁡(4,−9) are congruent. Similarity instead describes a change of coordinates for an operator and preserves its individual eigenvalues.
