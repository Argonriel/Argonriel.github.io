MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Dual basis and dual map

deflinear-algebra/duality

Let β=(v1,…,vn) be a basis of V. Its dual basis β∗=(f1,…,fn) satisfies fi(vj)=δij. Thus fi reads the ith coordinate: if v=∑ajvj, then fi(v)=ai.

For a basis matrix P=[v1 ⋯ vn] in Fn, fi is represented by row i of P−1. For the source's example, P=(1321),P−1=(−1/53/52/5−1/5). So f1(x,y)=(−x+3y)/5 and f2(x,y)=(2x−y)/5.

Dual map

For T:V→W, define T′:W∗→V∗ by T′(g)=g∘T.

If A=[T]βγ, then [T′]γ∗β∗=AT. This is an ordinary transpose even over C. The prime distinguishes this map from the inner-product adjoint T∗; the source uses a star for both contexts.

The evaluation map J:V→V∗∗, defined by J(v)(f)=f(v), is linear and injective. In finite dimension it is an isomorphism. Unlike a choice of V≅V∗, it requires no basis or inner product.
