MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Unitary and orthogonal operators

deflinear-algebra/inner-product

Let U be an operator on a finite-dimensional inner-product space.

Unitary / orthogonal

Over C, U is unitary if U∗U=UU∗=I. Over R, the corresponding term is orthogonal.

The following are equivalent in finite dimension: U∗U=I,U−1=U∗,⟨Ux,Uy⟩=⟨x,y⟩(x,y∈V). They are also equivalent to preservation of every norm ‖Ux‖=‖x‖, by polarization. Norm preservation gives injectivity; finite dimension gives surjectivity.

In orthonormal coordinates, the columns of a unitary matrix form an orthonormal basis. A unitary operator is normal; every eigenvalue has modulus 1, since ‖λx‖=‖x‖.

For a real orthogonal matrix Q, Q−1=QT and detQ=±1. The determinant alone is not a test for orthogonality.

Examples: plane rotations have determinant 1; reflection across a line has determinant −1. A rotation through an angle other than 0 or π has no real eigenvectors, despite preserving lengths.

Unitary similarity B=U∗AU preserves the inner product as well as the operator's algebraic structure. General similarity need not preserve lengths or angles.
