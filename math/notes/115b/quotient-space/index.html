MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Quotient space

defthmlinear-algebra/foundations

Let W be a subspace of V. The quotient space consists of cosets V/W={v+W:v∈V},v+W=u+W⟺v−u∈W. Define (u+W)+(v+W)=(u+v)+W and a(v+W)=av+W. These are independent of the chosen representatives because W is a subspace.

Quotient map

π:V→V/W,π(v)=v+W is linear and surjective, with ker⁡π=W.

The zero vector of V/W is the coset W, not a single vector of V.

If V is finite-dimensional, extend a basis of W to one of V. The cosets of the additional vectors form a basis of the quotient, so dim⁡(V/W)=dim⁡V−dim⁡W.

First isomorphism theorem

For a linear map T:V→U, V/ker⁡T≅im⁡T,v+ker⁡T⟼Tv.

The map is well-defined because equal cosets differ by a kernel vector; it is injective and surjective by construction. No finite-dimensional assumption is needed for this isomorphism.

Example: if W is the horizontal axis in R2, a coset records only the vertical coordinate. A quotient identifies vectors; it is not automatically an orthogonal complement.
