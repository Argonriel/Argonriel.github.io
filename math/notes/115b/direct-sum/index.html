MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Direct sum

deflinear-algebra/foundations

For subspaces U,W⊆V, U+W={u+w:u∈U, w∈W}. This is a subspace, generally different from the union U∪W.

Internal direct sum

V=U⊕W⟺V=U+W and U∩W={0}. Equivalently, each v∈V has a unique decomposition v=u+w.

Uniqueness follows because u+w=u′+w′ puts u−u′=w′−w in the intersection. Conversely, a nonzero vector in the intersection gives two decompositions.

If U,W are finite-dimensional, dim⁡(U+W)=dim⁡U+dim⁡W−dim⁡(U∩W). For a direct sum, bases of the summands concatenate to a basis of V.

Direct need not mean orthogonal: R2=span⁡(1,0)⊕span⁡(1,1). An orthogonal decomposition is a special case, with an inner product supplying the complement.

For more than two summands, require uniqueness of the whole sum, or Uj∩∑i≠jUi={0}. Pairwise zero intersections alone do not suffice: three distinct lines in R2 give a counterexample.
