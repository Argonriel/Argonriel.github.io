MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Cayley-Hamilton theorem

thmlinear-algebra/operators

Cayley-Hamilton

Every operator on a finite-dimensional vector space satisfies its characteristic polynomial: χT(T)=0.

Here χT(t)=det(tI−[T]). If χT(t)=tn+cn−1tn−1+⋯+c0, the statement means Tn+cn−1Tn−1+⋯+c0I=0. Constants become scalar multiples of I.

Proof route: for each v≠0, the restriction to Z(v,T) has characteristic polynomial pv and satisfies pv(T)v=0 by the companion-matrix description. Since this subspace is invariant, pv divides χT. Hence χT(T)v=0 for every v.

For a 2×2 matrix, A2−(tr⁡A)A+(detA)I=0. If detA≠0, this gives A−1=(tr⁡A)I−AdetA.

More generally, powers of T can be reduced to combinations of I,T,…,Tn−1. The minimal polynomial can make this reduction shorter.

The theorem works over any field. It does not assume that the characteristic polynomial splits or that T is diagonalizable.
