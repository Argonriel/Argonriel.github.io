MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Jordan canonical form

defthmlinear-algebra/operators

Existence over the chosen field

For A∈Mn(F), a Jordan form A=PJP−1 exists iff its characteristic polynomial splits over F.

Each block is Jr(λ)=λI+N, where N has ones immediately above the diagonal and zeros elsewhere.

A chain (v1,…,vr) satisfies (T−λI)v1=0,(T−λI)vj=vj−1(j≥2), with v1≠0. Its vectors are independent: applying successive powers of T−λI to a relation kills all but its last surviving coefficient. A basis made of such chains gives Jordan blocks.

For an eigenvalue λ:

  • the sum of block sizes is its algebraic multiplicity;
  • the number of blocks is dim⁡Eλ;
  • the largest block size is the exponent of t−λ in mT.

Recovering sizes: set d0=0 and dk=dim⁡ker⁡(T−λI)k. Then dk−dk−1=#{λ-blocks of size at least k}. The successive differences determine the blocks uniquely, up to order.

Example: a nilpotent operator on a five-dimensional space with (d1,d2,d3)=(2,4,5) has blocks of sizes 3 and 2. Here the kernels have already filled the whole space at k=3. Without the dimension or a stabilization condition, these three values would not rule out a larger block.

If N3=0 but N2≠0, the largest block has size exactly 3; this alone does not determine all other blocks.

Diagonalization is precisely the case where all Jordan blocks have size 1.
