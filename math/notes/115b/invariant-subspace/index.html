MATH 115B · Linear Algebra II

Invariant subspace

deflinear-algebra/operators

Let T:V→V be linear. A subspace W is T-invariant if T(W)⊆W.

This allows both a restriction T|W:W→W and a quotient operator T―:V/W→V/W,T―(v+W)=Tv+W. If v−u∈W, invariance ensures Tv−Tu∈W. Conversely, this formula is well-defined only if T(W)⊆W.

In finite dimension, extend a basis of W to a basis of V. Then [T]=(AC0B), where A represents T|W and B represents T― in the corresponding quotient basis. Hence χT(t)=χT|W(t)χT―(t).

The upper-right block need not vanish. An invariant subspace need not have an invariant complement: for T=(1101),W=span⁡(1,0), there is no second invariant line complementary to W.

Examples: eigenspaces, generalized eigenspaces and cyclic subspaces are invariant. For self-adjoint T, invariance of W also implies invariance of W⟂.
