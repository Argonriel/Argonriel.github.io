MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Convolution

defthmanalysis/fourier

Periodic setting

For f,g∈C(T,C), T=R/Z, define (f∗g)(x)=∫01f(x−t)g(t)dt=∫01f(t)g(x−t)dt. The equality follows by substitution and periodicity. Convolution is a weighted average of translates when g≥0 and ∫01g=1.

A positive approximate identity

Suppose continuous periodic kernels Kj satisfy Kj≥0,∫01Kj(t)dt=1, ∀η∈(0,1/2):∫η1−ηKj(t)dt⟶0. Then, for every f∈C(T,C), f∗Kj⇉f.

Concentration is near 0 on the circle, hence near both ends of the coordinate interval [0,1].

Proof estimate: uniform continuity gives |f(x−t)−f(x)|<ε whenever the circle distance from t to 0 is <η. Therefore ‖f∗Kj−f‖∞≤ε+2‖f‖∞∫η1−ηKj(t)dt.

Trig-polynomial stability: if K(t)=∑|n|≤Nbnen(t), then f∗K=∑|n|≤Nbnf^(n)en. This is the mechanism of Fourier theorem.

Real-line setting

For bounded uniformly continuous f:R→C and continuous compactly supported K, the integral (f∗K)(x)=∫Rf(x−t)K(t)dt exists. If Kj≥0, ∫RKj=1, and ∫|t|≥ηKj→0 for every η>0, the same estimate gives uniform convergence to f.

In particular this applies to continuous compactly supported f. It is not a uniform-convergence statement for arbitrary functions. Weierstrass Approximation Theorem specifies the extension and restricted interval needed for ordinary polynomial approximants.
