MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Taylor series

defanalysis/series

For f smooth on a neighborhood of c, its Taylor series at c is ∑n=0∞f(n)(c)n!(x−c)n. Forming this series does not assert convergence or equality with f.

First-order approximation

Differentiability at x gives f(x+h)=f(x)+f′(x)h+o(|h|). The stronger remainder O(h2) follows, for example, when f∈C2 near x (by the Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder). It does not follow from differentiability alone: f(h)=|h|3/2 at 0.

Analytic versus smooth

f is analytic at c if it equals a convergent power series on a neighborhood of c. Its coefficients then equal f(n)(c)/n!.

Smooth does not imply analytic

f(x)={e−1/|x|,x≠0,0,x=0. This function is C∞, all derivatives at 0 vanish, but f(x)>0 for x≠0. Its Taylor series at 0 is identically 0.

Reason: every derivative away from 0 is an exponential times a polynomial in 1/|x| (with side-dependent signs); tme−t→0 as t→∞ for every m. This also controls the difference quotients at 0.

Example: ex=∑n=0∞xn/n!, with radius +∞.
