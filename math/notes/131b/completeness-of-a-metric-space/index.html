MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Completeness of a metric space

defthmanalysis/metric

Definition

A metric space (X,d) is complete if every Cauchy sequence in X converges to a point of X.

R, Rd are complete; Q is not. Completeness generalizes the Completeness Axiom for R.

Bounded functions with the sup metric

For a nonempty set S, define B(S,R)={f:S→R:f bounded},d∞(f,g)=supx∈S|f(x)−g(x)|.

Theorem

(B(S,R),d∞) is complete.

Proof: if (fn) is sup-Cauchy, then (fn(x)) is real-Cauchy for each x, so define f(x)=limnfn(x). Given ε>0, choose N so that n,m≥N gives supS|fn−fm|<ε/2. Let m→∞ pointwise: |fn(x)−f(x)|≤ε/2∀x, n≥N. Thus fn⇉f. Taking one sufficiently late fN gives |f(x)|≤|fN(x)|+1, so f is bounded.

Subspaces

  • A closed subset of a complete metric space is complete.
  • A complete metric subspace is closed in its ambient metric space: a convergent sequence from the subspace is Cauchy, and uniqueness identifies its limit with the subspace limit.
  • Every compact metric space is complete: extract a convergent subsequence of a Cauchy sequence, then use Cauchy to bring the whole sequence to the same limit.

Complete does not imply compact; an infinite discrete metric space is a counterexample.
