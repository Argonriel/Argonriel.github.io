Let f∈C(T,C), T=R/Z, and FN=∑|n|≤Nf^(n)en.

Finite orthogonal identities

By orthonormality and projection, ‖FN‖22=∑|n|≤N|f^(n)|2, ‖f‖22=‖FN‖22+‖f−FN‖22. In particular, ∑|n|≤N|f^(n)|2≤‖f‖22 (Bessel's inequality).

Passing to the limit

For ε>0, Fourier theorem gives a trig polynomial P∈VM with ‖f−P‖∞<ε. For N≥M, the projection minimizes L2 error: ‖f−FN‖2≤‖f−P‖2≤‖f−P‖∞<ε. Therefore FN→f in L2, and

Parseval

∫01|f(x)|2dx=‖f‖22=∑n∈Z|f^(n)|2.

Meaning: energy in the function equals energy in its Fourier coefficients. The argument uses L2 convergence of FN, not uniform convergence.
