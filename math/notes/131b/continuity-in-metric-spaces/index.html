MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Continuity in metric spaces

defthmanalysis/continuity

Let f:(X,dX)→(Y,dY) and fix x0∈X.

At one point

The following are equivalent:

  1. Epsilon–delta: ∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 ∀x∈X:dX(x,x0)<δ⟹dY(f(x),f(x0))<ε.
  2. Sequential: for every sequence (xn) in X, xn→x0⟹f(xn)→f(x0).

δ=δ(ε,x0) may depend on the fixed base point. Reversing the order to choose one δ before all base points is Uniform continuity.

On the whole space

Open-preimage criterion

f is continuous at every point of X iff f−1(U) is open in X for every open U⊆Y.

Proof direction: if x∈f−1(U), choose a ball around f(x) contained in U; continuity gives a ball around x contained in its preimage. Conversely, take U=BY(f(x0),ε) and use openness of its preimage.

It is the preimage that stays open. A continuous map need not send open sets to open sets: a constant map R→R sends R to a singleton.

Connections: Lipschitz continuity ⇒ Uniform continuity ⇒ continuity. Compact domains give the middle condition by Heine–Cantor.
