MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Weierstrass Approximation Theorem

thmanalysis/fourier

Theorem

Let a<b be finite. For f∈C([a,b],R) and every ε>0, there is an ordinary real polynomial P such that supx∈[a,b]|f(x)−P(x)|<ε.

Polynomials are dense in C([a,b]) under the sup norm.

Landau-kernel proof map

The source board sketches convolution and polynomial approximation. The following makes the endpoint extension and domain restrictions explicit.

  1. Rescale to I=[−1/2,1/2]. Subtract the linear interpolant ℓ of f at the endpoints. Then g=f−ℓ vanishes at both endpoints, so extending g by 0 outside I gives a continuous compactly supported function.
  2. Set Kn(t)=cn(1−t2)n1[−1,1](t),cn−1=∫−11(1−t2)ndt(n≥1). These nonnegative kernels have mass 1 and concentrate at 0. For 0<η<1, ∫|t|≥ηKn(t)dt≤2η(1−η21−η2/4)n→0.
  3. For x∈I, every y∈I has |x−y|≤1, so (g∗Kn)(x)=cn∫Ig(y)(1−(x−y)2)ndy=:Pn(x). The last expression defines a polynomial in x (degree at most 2n).
  4. By approximate identity, Pn→g uniformly on I. Add ℓ and undo the affine rescaling.

The cutoff kernel is not a polynomial on all of R; the restricted interval in step 3 is what makes the approximant polynomial.
