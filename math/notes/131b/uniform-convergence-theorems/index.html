MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Uniform convergence theorems

thmanalysis/functions

All functions below are real-valued; ⇉ means uniform convergence on the stated common domain. Continuity preservation is proved in Uniform convergence.

Boundedness and uniform continuity

  • Bounded fn, fn⇉f ⟹f bounded: choose one n with ‖f−fn‖∞<1.
  • Uniformly continuous fn on a metric space, fn⇉f ⟹f uniformly continuous: choose one n for two ε/3 approximation errors, then use its uniform continuity for the middle term.

Integration on a finite interval

If each fn is Riemann integrable on [a,b], a<b finite, and fn⇉f, then f is Riemann integrable and |∫abfn−∫abf|≤(b−a)‖fn−f‖∞→0. For integrability, compare Darboux sums: U(f,P)−L(f,P)≤U(fn,P)−L(fn,P)+2(b−a)‖f−fn‖∞. Choose n first, then P.

Differentiation

A sufficient theorem

Let fn∈C1([a,b]), a<b finite. If fn′⇉g and fn(x0)→ℓ for one x0∈[a,b], then fn⇉f, where f(x)=ℓ+∫x0xg(t)dt,f′=g on (a,b).

Proof: g is continuous by uniform convergence, and FTC gives ‖fn−f‖∞≤|fn(x0)−ℓ|+(b−a)‖fn′−g‖∞→0.

Why the assumptions matter

  • fn⇉f alone need not preserve differentiability: x2+1/n⇉|x| on [−1,1].
  • Without convergence at one point, constants fn=n have fn′=0 but no function limit.
  • On an unbounded interval, global uniform convergence of fn need not follow: fn(x)=x/n on R has fn′⇉0 and fn(0)=0, but fn does not converge uniformly. The corresponding conclusion is uniform convergence on each compact interval.
