MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

thmanalysis/integration

Let a<b be finite and f∈C([a,b],R).

Part 1

If F(x)=∫axf(t)dt, then F is continuous on [a,b], differentiable on (a,b), and F′(x)=f(x)(a<x<b). At the endpoints the corresponding one-sided derivatives equal f(a),f(b).

Proof idea: F(x+h)−F(x)h−f(x)=1h∫xx+h(f(t)−f(x))dt, whose absolute value is bounded by the oscillation of f between x and x+h, tending to 0.

Part 2

If G is continuous on [a,b], differentiable on (a,b), and G′=f there, then ∫abf(x)dx=G(b)−G(a).

Proof: Part 1 gives F′=f, so (G−F)′=0. MVT makes G−F constant.
