Uniform density of trigonometric polynomials

For f∈C(T,C), T=R/Z, and every ε>0, there is a trigonometric polynomial P with ‖f−P‖∞<ε.

Fejér approximation

An explicit choice comes from the Fejér kernel KN(t)=1N+1|∑k=0Ne2πikt|2=∑|n|≤N(1−|n|N+1)e2πint. It is nonnegative, has integral 1, and its mass away from 0 on the circle tends to 0. Thus Convolution gives σNf=f∗KN=∑|n|≤N(1−|n|N+1)f^(n)en⇉f.

If Fj is the ordinary jth Fourier partial sum, then σNf=(N+1)−1∑j=0NFj.

Distinction: this proves uniform convergence of the Fejér means, not of the ordinary partial sums FN. Continuity alone does not guarantee uniform convergence of FN.

Connection to L2: for continuous periodic functions, ‖f−P‖2≤‖f−P‖∞. Combining density with the best-approximation property of FN yields FN→f in L2 and Parseval identity. Extending density to all of Lebesgue L2 additionally uses density of continuous functions there.

The ordinary-polynomial counterpart is Weierstrass Approximation Theorem.
