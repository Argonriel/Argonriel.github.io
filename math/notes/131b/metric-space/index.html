MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Metric space

defanalysis/metric

Definition

A metric space (X,d) has a finite-valued distance d:X×X→[0,∞) satisfying d(x,y)=0⟺x=y,d(x,y)=d(y,x), d(x,z)≤d(x,y)+d(y,z).

Open ball: BX(x,r)={y∈X:d(x,y)<r}, for r>0.

Space Metric Required domain
R |x−y| Real numbers
Rd ∑j(xj−yj)2 Finite-dimensional Euclidean space
B(S,R) supx∈S|f(x)−g(x)| Bounded functions on a nonempty set S
C(R/Z,C) (∫01|f−g|2)1/2 Continuous periodic functions; see L2 space

For arbitrary functions the sup can be infinite. For arbitrary integrable functions, L2 distance can be zero between functions that differ at individual points; continuity or an almost-everywhere equivalence relation resolves this.

What generalizes: the definitions of convergence, Cauchy sequences, open/closed sets, continuity, and compactness use d in place of Euclidean distance.

What needs extra hypotheses:

  • Cauchy ⇒ convergent requires completeness.
  • Closed and bounded ⇒ compact is the Euclidean Heine–Borel theorem, not a general metric-space fact.

Counterexample: an infinite discrete space, d(x,y)=1 for x≠y, is bounded and complete but not compact: its singleton open cover has no finite subcover.
