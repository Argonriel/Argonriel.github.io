Course setting: continuous periodic functions

The source notes work with f,g∈C(T,C), where T=R/Z. Define ⟨f,g⟩=∫01f(x)g(x)―dx, ‖f‖2=(∫01|f|2)1/2,d2(f,g)=‖f−g‖2. The inner product is linear in its first argument. Continuity ensures positive definiteness: if a continuous f is nonzero at a point, |f| stays positive on a small interval, so ∫|f|2>0.

Cauchy–Schwarz from Riemann integral yields |⟨f,g⟩|≤‖f‖2‖g‖2 and the triangle inequality.

Comparison with the sup norm (interval length 1): ‖f−g‖2≤‖f−g‖∞. Uniform convergence implies L2 convergence. The converse fails: narrow continuous spikes can have height 1 but area tending to 0.

For example, on [0,1] periodically extended, fn(x)=max{1−n|x−1/2|,0}(n≥2) has ‖fn‖∞=1 and ‖fn‖22=2/(3n)→0.

Terminology boundary

The full Lebesgue space L2(T) consists of square-integrable measurable functions modulo equality almost everywhere. Without this identification, changing one point gives distance 0 between distinct functions. It is a complete space; C(T) equipped with ‖⋅‖2 is not complete.

The existing Fourier cards state their results for the course's continuous-periodic setting; building the full Lebesgue theory is outside this review.
