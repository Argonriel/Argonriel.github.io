MATH 131B · Real Analysis II

Power series

defthmanalysis/series

For real x,c and real or complex coefficients, consider ∑n=0∞an(x−c)n.

Radius of convergence

R=1lim supn→∞|an|1/n,1/0=+∞,1/(+∞)=0. By Root test:

  • |x−c|<R: absolute convergence.
  • |x−c|>R: divergence.
  • For 0<R<∞, check each endpoint c−R,c+R separately.
  • At x=c, the series always converges to a0, including when R=0.

Inside the interval of convergence

On |x−c|≤r<R, choose r<ρ<R (for r=0 the conclusion is immediate). Convergence at radius ρ bounds |an|ρn, giving a geometric majorant on radius r. The Weierstrass M-test yields uniform convergence.

If R>0, the sum f is continuous and f′(x)=∑n=1∞nan(x−c)n−1, ∫cxf(t)dt=∑n=0∞ann+1(x−c)n+1(|x−c|<R). The differentiated series has the same radius and converges uniformly on smaller closed intervals; apply Uniform convergence theorems. Uniform convergence of the original series alone would not justify differentiation.
