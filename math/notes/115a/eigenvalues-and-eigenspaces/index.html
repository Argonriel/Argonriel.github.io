MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Eigenvalues and eigenspaces

deflinear-algebra/operators

Let T:V→V be linear and finite-dimensional over F.

Eigenvector and eigenspace

A nonzero v is an eigenvector with eigenvalue λ∈F if Tv=λv. Eλ=ker⁡(T−λI).

The eigenspace includes 0; an eigenvector does not.

Using the monic convention, the characteristic polynomial is χT(t)=det(tI−[T]β). It is basis-independent, has degree n=dim⁡V, and λ is an eigenvalue iff χT(λ)=0. The convention det([T]β−tI) differs by (−1)n.

Multiplicities: geometric multiplicity is dim⁡Eλ; algebraic multiplicity mλ is the multiplicity of λ as a root of χT. 1≤dim⁡Eλ≤mλfor an eigenvalue λ.

Eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues are independent. To see the induction step, apply T−λkI to a shortest alleged dependence.

Field matters: a 90∘ rotation has no real eigenvalues, but has eigenvalues i,−i over C. If Tv=λv, then p(T)v=p(λ)v for every polynomial p.
