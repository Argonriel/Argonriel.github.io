MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Vector space

deflinear-algebra/foundations

Let F be a field. The course mainly uses R and C.

Definition

A vector space over F is a set V with addition and scalar multiplication such that (V,+) is an abelian group and, for a,b∈F and u,v∈V, a(u+v)=au+av,(a+b)v=av+bv, a(bv)=(ab)v,1v=v.

The operations take values in V. The zero vector belongs to V; 0v=0V and (−1)v=−v follow from the axioms.

Examples: Fn, matrices of a fixed size, polynomials, and functions X→F with pointwise operations: (f+g)(x)=f(x)+g(x),(af)(x)=af(x).

The scalar field matters: C has dimension 1 over C and dimension 2 over R. A vector need not be an arrow; functions are vectors too.

Field reminder: 0≠1, and every nonzero scalar has a multiplicative inverse. This qualification is needed in the source's list of field axioms.
