MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Normal and self-adjoint operators

defthmlinear-algebra/operators

Let T be an operator on a finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product space.

T self-adjoint⟺T=T∗,T normal⟺TT∗=T∗T. Every self-adjoint operator is normal. In orthonormal coordinates, self-adjoint matrices are real symmetric or complex Hermitian.

Normality and eigenvectors

If T is normal, then Tx=λx⟺T∗x=λ―x.

Proof idea: for normal S, ‖Sx‖=‖S∗x‖. Apply this to the normal operator S=T−λI. Its kernel equals that of S∗.

If Tx=λx and Ty=μy with λ≠μ, then normality gives λ⟨x,y⟩=⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T∗y⟩=μ⟨x,y⟩, so x⟂y.

A self-adjoint operator has real eigenvalues: λ‖x‖2=⟨Tx,x⟩ is real for an eigenvector x.

Counterexample to a converse: multiplication by i on C is normal but not self-adjoint. A real plane rotation is normal but need not have real eigenvectors. The field distinction is essential in the Spectral theorem.
