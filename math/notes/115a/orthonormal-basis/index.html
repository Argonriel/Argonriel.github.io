MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Orthonormal basis

deflinear-algebra/inner-product

A list (e1,…,en) is orthonormal if ⟨ei,ej⟩=δij. Every orthonormal list is independent: take the inner product of ∑ajej=0 with ek to obtain ak=0.

Coordinates in an orthonormal basis

If the list is a basis of V, then v=∑j=1n⟨v,ej⟩ej. The order of the inner-product arguments uses the convention “linear in the first argument.”

For x=∑ajej and y=∑bjej, ⟨x,y⟩=∑jajbj―,‖x‖2=∑j|aj|2. Thus orthonormal coordinates preserve both linear structure and the inner product.

If the list does not span V, the sum gives the projection of v, not necessarily v itself; then ∑j|⟨v,ej⟩|2≤‖v‖2.

The finite identity is the starting point for Parseval identity. Passing to an infinite family requires convergence and completeness/density arguments, not just the finite coordinate calculation.
