MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Orthogonal complement

defthmlinear-algebra/inner-product

For any subset S of an inner-product space V, S⟂={x∈V:⟨x,s⟩=0 for every s∈S}. It is always a subspace, even if S is not a subspace or is infinite.

{0}⟂=V,V⟂={0},S⟂=(span⁡S)⟂. Also S⊆(S⟂)⟂, and S⊆R implies R⟂⊆S⟂.

Orthogonal decomposition

If W⊆V is a finite-dimensional subspace, then V=W⊕W⟂. Every v has a unique decomposition v=w+z, with w∈W, z∈W⟂.

Choose an orthonormal basis of W and take w=∑j⟨v,ej⟩ej. Then v−w is perpendicular to every basis vector. Uniqueness follows from W∩W⟂={0}.

If V is finite-dimensional, then dim⁡W⟂=dim⁡V−dim⁡W,(S⟂)⟂=span⁡S. Do not apply the last identity to arbitrary infinite-dimensional spaces without further hypotheses. The definition of S⟂ itself needs no finite-dimensional assumption.
