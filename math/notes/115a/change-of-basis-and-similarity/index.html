MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Change of basis and similarity

deflinear-algebra/operators

Let T:V→V with dim⁡V=n, and let β,γ be ordered bases. Put P=[[γ1]β⋯[γn]β]. Then [v]β=P[v]γ: P converts new coordinates to old coordinates.

Similarity

If A=[T]β and B=[T]γ, then B=P−1AP.

Apply P to enter old coordinates, apply A, then P−1 to return. This order prevents most change-of-basis mistakes.

Similar matrices have the same characteristic and minimal polynomials, determinant, trace, rank, nullity and eigenvalue multiplicities.

Their kernels and images need not be the same coordinate subsets: ker⁡B=P−1(ker⁡A),im⁡B=P−1(im⁡A). For example, swapping the coordinates changes the kernel of diag⁡(0,1) from the first coordinate axis to the second.

When domain and codomain bases change independently, the general rule is Anew=Q−1AP. Similarity uses the same change on both sides. A quadratic form instead changes by congruence PTAP.
