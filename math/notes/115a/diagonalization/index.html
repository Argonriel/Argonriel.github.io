MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Diagonalization

defthmlinear-algebra/operators

Definition

T∈L(V) is diagonalizable over F if V has a basis of eigenvectors of T.

For a matrix A, place these eigenvectors in the columns of P. Then A=PDP−1,D=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn). The jth diagonal entry corresponds to the jth column of P.

For n=dim⁡V<∞, the following are equivalent:

  • T is diagonalizable over F;
  • V is the direct sum of its eigenspaces;
  • ∑λdim⁡Eλ=n;
  • χT splits over F and dim⁡Eλ=mλ for every eigenvalue.

Having n distinct eigenvalues is sufficient, not necessary: In is diagonalizable with only one eigenvalue when n>1.

Failure example: A=(1101),E1=span⁡{(1,0)}. Its characteristic polynomial is (t−1)2, but it has only one independent eigenvector. This nontrivial shear needs a Jordan block.

Diagonalization does not generally give an orthonormal eigenbasis; that stronger conclusion is governed by the Spectral theorem.
