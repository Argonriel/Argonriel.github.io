MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Gram-Schmidt

thmlinear-algebra/inner-product

Start with an independent list (v1,…,vn) in a real or complex inner-product space. Define successively uk=vk−∑j<k⟨vk,ej⟩ej,ek=uk‖uk‖. For k=1, the sum is empty.

What the algorithm preserves

The resulting list is orthonormal, and for every k, span⁡(e1,…,ek)=span⁡(v1,…,vk).

Why it works: subtracting the projection makes uk orthogonal to the previous ej. If uk=0, then vk lies in the earlier span, contradicting independence. Normalization is therefore valid.

Example: from v1=(1,1), v2=(1,0) in R2, e1=(1,1)2,u2=(1/2,−1/2),e2=(1,−1)2.

For a dependent input, a residual may be zero; skip it rather than divide by zero. Every finite-dimensional inner-product space consequently has an orthonormal basis.

If the residuals are kept unnormalized, the subtraction coefficients must be ⟨vk,uj⟩/‖uj‖2. Do not mix the normalized and unnormalized formulas.
