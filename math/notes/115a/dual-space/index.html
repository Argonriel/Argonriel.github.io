MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Dual space

deflinear-algebra/duality

For a vector space V over F, its algebraic dual is V∗=L(V,F), the space of all linear functionals. A functional takes a vector as input and returns a scalar.

Examples include coordinate functionals, evaluation f↦f(a), and trace on Mn(F). Determinant is generally not a linear functional.

In finite dimension, dim⁡V∗=dim⁡V; the coordinate-reading functionals associated with a basis form its dual basis.

Inner-product representation (finite dimension)

If V is a finite-dimensional inner-product space, every φ∈V∗ has a unique representing vector y such that φ(x)=⟨x,y⟩(x∈V).

For an orthonormal basis (ej), take y=∑jφ(ej)―ej. This proves existence; testing the difference of two representing vectors proves uniqueness.

With the first-argument-linear convention, y↦⟨⋅,y⟩ is conjugate-linear over C, and linear over R. It is not a canonical complex-linear identification V→V∗.

In infinite dimension, the algebraic dual and the continuous dual need not coincide. Do not extend the finite-dimensional representation statement to every algebraic functional.
