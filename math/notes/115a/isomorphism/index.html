MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Isomorphism

deflinear-algebra/maps

Definition

An isomorphism T:V→W is a bijective linear map. Write V≅W when one exists.

Its inverse is linear. Indeed, if T(u)=x and T(v)=y, then T(au+bv)=ax+by, so T−1(ax+by)=aT−1x+bT−1y.

The inverse identities have different domains: T−1T=IV,TT−1=IW. Having only a left or a right inverse does not generally imply both.

For finite-dimensional spaces over the same field, V≅W⟺dim⁡V=dim⁡W. Choose bases of equal length and map corresponding basis vectors to one another. Such an isomorphism need not be canonical.

Examples: the coordinate map V→Fn and Pm(F)≅Fm+1 via coefficients. The spaces have the same linear structure, but their elements need not be the same objects.
