MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Inner product

deflinear-algebra/inner-product

Let V be over F=R or C.

Convention: linear in the first argument

An inner product ⟨⋅,⋅⟩:V×V→F satisfies ⟨ax+by,z⟩=a⟨x,z⟩+b⟨y,z⟩, ⟨x,y⟩=⟨y,x⟩―, ⟨x,x⟩≥0,⟨x,x⟩=0⟺x=0.

Thus the second argument is conjugate-linear: ⟨x,ay+bz⟩=a―⟨x,y⟩+b―⟨x,z⟩. This agrees with the convention already used in L2 space.

Examples: ⟨x,y⟩=∑jxjyj―on Cn, ⟨f,g⟩=∫abf(t)g(t)―dton C([a,b],C), a<b.

The induced norm and metric are ‖x‖=⟨x,x⟩,d(x,y)=‖x−y‖. Their triangle inequality follows from Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Orthogonality means ⟨x,y⟩=0.

Positive definiteness includes ⟨0,0⟩=0; “strictly positive for every vector” would be incorrect. Not every norm comes from an inner product: such norms must satisfy the parallelogram identity.
