MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Adjoint

deflinear-algebra/inner-product

Let V,W be finite-dimensional inner-product spaces over R or C, and let T:V→W be linear.

Adjoint

The unique linear map T∗:W→V satisfying ⟨Tx,y⟩W=⟨x,T∗y⟩V(x∈V, y∈W) is the adjoint of T.

Existence follows by representing the functional x↦⟨Tx,y⟩ for each y; see Dual space.

Rules (with compatible domains): (S+T)∗=S∗+T∗,(aT)∗=a―T∗, (ST)∗=T∗S∗,T∗∗=T.

For A∈Mm×n(C), its conjugate transpose is A∗=A―T∈Mn×m(C).

In orthonormal bases β,γ, [T∗]γβ=([T]βγ)∗. An arbitrary nonorthonormal basis does not give this formula without accounting for the inner product.

Useful identities are ker⁡T∗=(im⁡T)⟂ and ker⁡T=(im⁡T∗)⟂. For an operator, detT∗=detT―.

The algebraic dual map acts on functionals and uses a transpose, with no complex conjugation. It is a different construction.
