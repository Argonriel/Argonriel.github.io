Spectral theorem

thmlinear-algebra/operators

Finite-dimensional spectral theorem

Over C, an operator has an orthonormal eigenbasis iff it is normal.

Over R, an operator has an orthonormal eigenbasis iff it is self-adjoint.

In matrix form: A∈Mn(C),AA∗=A∗A⟺A=UDU∗,U∗U=I, with D diagonal. If A is Hermitian, all entries of D are real. A∈Mn(R),A=AT⟺A=QDQT,QTQ=I, with D real diagonal.

Proof route: over C, choose an eigenvector. For a normal operator it is also an eigenvector of the adjoint, so its orthogonal complement is invariant under both T and T∗. The restriction is normal; induct on dimension. For real self-adjoint operators, complexification has real eigenvalues and yields a real eigenvector, after which the same induction applies.

Equivalently, for a normal complex operator (or self-adjoint real operator), T=∑λλPEλ,I=∑λPEλ, where the eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal.

Boundary: a real rotation can be normal without real diagonalization. General diagonalizability need not allow an orthonormal eigenbasis.
