Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

thmlinear-algebra/inner-product

Cauchy-Schwarz

In a real or complex inner-product space, |⟨x,y⟩|≤‖x‖‖y‖. Equality holds iff x,y are linearly dependent, including the case where one is zero.

Proof: for y≠0, subtract the component along y: 0≤‖x−⟨x,y⟩‖y‖2y‖2=‖x‖2−|⟨x,y⟩|2‖y‖2. Equality means the residual is zero. The case y=0 is immediate.

It implies the triangle inequality by ‖x+y‖2=‖x‖2+2Re⁡⟨x,y⟩+‖y‖2≤(‖x‖+‖y‖)2.

Every inner-product norm also satisfies ‖x+y‖2+‖x−y‖2=2‖x‖2+2‖y‖2.

In a real inner-product space, for nonzero x,y, define the angle by cos⁡θ=⟨x,y⟩/(‖x‖‖y‖). The same formula without taking account of complex values is not a real angle formula on a complex space.

Applying the inequality to the integral inner product gives the version used in Riemann integral and L2 space.
