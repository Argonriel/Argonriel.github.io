MATH 115A · Linear Algebra I

Orthogonal projection

defthmlinear-algebra/inner-product

Let W be a finite-dimensional subspace of an inner-product space V.

Orthogonal projection

PWv is the unique vector in W such that v−PWv∈W⟂.

For an orthonormal basis (e1,…,em) of W, PWv=∑j=1m⟨v,ej⟩ej. For an orthogonal basis of nonzero vectors (uj), divide each coefficient by ‖uj‖2 instead. A merely nonorthogonal basis does not permit either formula.

Best approximation

For every w∈W, ‖v−w‖2=‖v−PWv‖2+‖PWv−w‖2.

The two terms are orthogonal. Hence PWv is the unique closest vector in W to v.

PW is linear and satisfies PW2=PW, im⁡PW=W, ker⁡PW=W⟂ and ‖PWv‖≤‖v‖. It is self-adjoint.

Caution: idempotence alone does not mean orthogonal projection: P=(1100),P2=P,PT≠P. In finite-dimensional inner-product spaces, an operator is an orthogonal projection iff P2=P and P∗=P.

Fourier partial sums are projections onto finite spans of exponentials. Their best-approximation property uses this same identity.
