Simple rules, long memories: I study how nonlinear systems move energy around — and how to simulate them without lying about it.

The FPUT problem In progress

In 1955, Fermi, Pasta, Ulam and Tsingou fed energy into the lowest mode of a chain of nonlinearly coupled oscillators, expecting it to spread out and thermalize. Instead the energy wandered through a few low modes and then — almost entirely — came back. That near-recurrence opened a line of questions about integrability, solitons, and the slow road to equipartition that is still being walked today.

I'm studying this recurrence as a sole-author project: reproducing the classical FPUT-α experiment with symplectic integrators — schemes that preserve the geometric structure of Hamiltonian flow, so energy errors stay bounded over the very long integration times the problem demands — and examining how recurrence quality depends on lattice size, nonlinearity, and integration scheme.

Concrete results, figures, and write-up will appear here once finalized.

Live simulation: an FPUT-α chain (N = 32, α = 0.25) integrated with velocity Verlet — a symplectic scheme. All energy starts in mode 1; watch it leak into modes 2–6 and drift back.
